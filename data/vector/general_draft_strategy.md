# Draft Preparation Strategy

Have a plan. Fantasy drafts are complex battlegrounds, and if you go in without a solid strategy, you'll end up panic-picking by Round 6. Get to know the relevant rankings, average draft position (ADP), positional scarcity, league tendencies and everything you can to build a solid blueprint for the chaos to come.

Be familiar with the new NFL landscape. Have you factored in the fantasy implications of coaching changes, player movements, and team situations? If you haven't paid careful attention to the league since January, you might not notice some of the more fantasy-relevant comings and goings, and that will be to your detriment.

But don't overplan. Take-lock strategies are a surefire way to set yourself up for "draft tilt," when someone inevitably snipes your player or derails your plan. Be fluid and flexible, let the draft come to you, and then lean on your preparation to help you execute in the moment.

# Volume-Based Drafting Strategy

Follow the volume. Last year, the top five running backs in scrimmage touches all finished in the top seven at the position in fantasy points. All six of the wideouts with 150-plus targets were top 10 in fantasy by season's end. Opportunity is king in fantasy football. Finding the rosters where opportunity tells a promising story is a key component of smart drafting.

Load up on running backs early. You need to go into your fantasy football draft knowing who the running backs are that will get the volume and which guys are not in a minimal committee. More and more often, we see coaches implementing running backs by committee, and getting that solid workhorse back is crucial to your success. A workhorse running back should get at least 70% of the workload.

Always aim for the top of the depth chart and get the big volume and target guys. Never settle for someone who is or could be behind someone on the depth chart. Draft a player with less consistency that is WR1 on their team over a guy that is better known and is a WR2 on his team.

# Ascending Offense Strategy

Invest in ascending offenses. Everyone knows the top teams will churn out points and yards, and their star players are priced accordingly. The real value comes from upside offenses primed to outperform expectations. Last year, the Falcons and Commanders were far better on offense than they had been in 2023, giving us massive value relative to draft cost.

Don't fear bad offenses in all cases. Landing the high-volume anchors of iffy offenses can be an underrated consolation. In 2024, Alvin Kamara was the PPR RB9 on one of the league's worst offenses. Three-down running backs are the best pieces to target on these teams.

# Quarterback Draft Strategy

Go for "elite" or "fringe" players at quarterback. Both quarterback and tight end have sifted into similar tiers: a small, elite group at the top, a smattering of known commodities with question marks after that, and then a few discount values at the fringe. Avoid the middle group at QB - either snag the Elite Five (top tier) or wait on someone like Dak Prescott or Justin Fields several rounds later.

Anchor your team with an ace QB. Always make sure you have a solid QB that puts up points every week. These guys tend to put up the most points every single year, and there is much less volatility than other positions. Don't be afraid to double up and get a backup QB - draft a quarterback as early as the 5th round.

Be very careful with rookie or second-year QBs who had breakout years, as they have not yet proven they can deliver year in and out. Secure and anchor yourself with a proven QB and back him up with an upside QB later in the draft.

# Running Back Committee Strategy

Don't avoid RBBCs (running back by committee). In 2024, Miami, Detroit, the Los Angeles Chargers and Cincinnati produced five top-24 running backs despite unclear RB depth charts. Both De'Von Achane (RB5) and Chase Brown (RB10) had fewer than 300 total touches. Running back committees are the way of the modern NFL, and we have to evolve.

Handcuff running backs from other managers' teams. This is the simplest way to follow league-winning upside. Injuries are unfortunately common in this sport, and a handful of backups will be starting by October. If you read this tip last year, you might have picked up Jordan Mason before CMC's two-month absence.

# Tight End Draft Strategy

Wait on tight ends until after the 6th round. Tight ends are essential but NOT as important as different positions such as running back early on. After grabbing a safe TE in rounds 6-7, get a TE with upside at round 10 or later. This way you have a TE that could potentially break out and a safe one in mid-rounds.

Go for "elite" or "fringe" players at tight end. Either lock in the Elite Three (Brock Bowers, Trey McBride, George Kittle) or wait for players like Evan Engram or rookie Tyler Warren several rounds later, while happily passing on the middle tier.

There are only a few consistent tight ends each year. You're better off waiting and grabbing a rookie TE mid to later rounds with upside. Be happy with about 10 points PPR out of your TE every game.

# Late Round Strategy and Sleepers

Stuff your bench with league-winning upside. It's usually better to shoot for upside in the unknowns rather than known commodities. Aim to draft potential league-winners. Last year, if you picked Bucky Irving over Khalil Herbert in deep rounds, you got the RB13 instead of the RB99.

Target rookies. Last year continued the ever-blossoming trend of rookie relevance, with three rookie wide receivers, two rookie quarterbacks and a rookie tight end all finishing top-12 at their positions in Year 1. The league has been steadily shifting in this direction, and fantasy managers need to take notice.

In later rounds, secure some rookies with major upside. Look at the depth chart and project volume and opportunity for any running back you decide to draft. Sometimes you may want to snipe a rookie RB in earlier rounds if you believe the upside and opportunity are there.

# Risk Management and Balance

Don't buy the "injury-prone" narrative. With rare exceptions, players are not injury-prone just because they've missed games in multiple seasons. Many NFL injuries are freak accidents, not the product of brittle bones. James Conner earned that label after seven incomplete seasons, then played 16 games and finished as the RB11 last season.

Balance your roster and have tons of depth. You need a balance of players that have upside and players that have consistency and are safe. In rounds 1-4, you want the safer, more proven talent. Take chances on upside and rookies in mid to late rounds. Ensure you have depth at all positions to accommodate for bye weeks, lack of performance, and injuries.

Don't run from risk, embrace it. If you're gunning for the championship, then listen up: This is a game of risks, and you have to take big shots to win big prizes. Whether it's the sleeper you snag two rounds early or the blockbuster trade you make in Week 2, we're not here to "play it safe."

# Positional Cliffs and Backup Plans

Don't pass the positional cliffs without drafting at least one relatively safe player. At each position, there's a "cliff" beyond which you're going to feel very uncomfortable taking your highest-rated remaining option. For many, they occur after RB18, WR20, TE8 and QB14. If you can have one of each position by Round 7 or 8, you'll leave your draft feeling much more stable.

Have a backup plan for a backup plan. When in doubt and panic mode, draft the best available player for the position you most need to fill. If you have made a list of players you have to have based on your research, work down that list. Late-round steals can sometimes be the league winners.

# CUDDY Drafting System

Use the "CUDDY" system before drafting any player. Make sure any player on your team has at least 3 out of 5 CUDDY criteria met:

**Consistency**: The player must put up points year in and year out, week in and week out. Look for players that are super consistent and produce often.

**Upside**: The player with high ceiling and upside. Upside is what wins championships in fantasy football, though with upside comes significant risk.

**Durability**: Stray away from injury-prone players and always aim to land durable and consistent players whenever possible.

**Depth**: Never settle for someone who is or could be behind someone on the depth chart. Always aim for the top of the depth chart and get the big volume guys.

**Youth**: Rookies coming in are hungry to succeed and leave their mark. Especially target rookie RBs landing on teams where they will get volume as your RB3-4 after securing proven workhorse backs.

# League Rules and ADP Knowledge

Know the league rules inside and out. You have to know PPR vs standard scoring, roster positions, league size, draft format, and timing between picks. Understanding the league rules will play a massive part in the drafting process. As a rule of thumb, always draft 2 QBs in a single QB league, and 3 QBs in a two quarterback league.

Know the ADPs (Average Draft Position) of players. You must make a move and draft that player before someone else does. You need to anticipate when a player may be taken off the board. The secret to avoiding getting sniped is knowing when players are coming off the board on average. Mock drafting is the best way to gauge when players are being drafted.

# Avoiding Common Biases

Don't let recency bias reign. Flush the "what has a player done for me lately" mindset. Situations change, injuries heal and talent usually wins out. Last year, Josh Jacobs and Aaron Jones were bounce-back candidates who both thrived in their new homes with top-15 finishes among running backs.

Don't let "schedule difficulty" sway your decisions. The NFL is ever-changing. Don't let a player's "strength of schedule" -- whether enticing or daunting -- significantly shift their place in your rankings. In 2023, the Cowboys defense was stingy; in 2024, they allowed the second-most points in the league.

Be afraid to buck the trends. "Consensus" does not always mean "right" or even "best." Six straight consensus 1.01 picks have missed at least five games in the ensuing season. If there's anything we know for sure in fantasy football, it's that we don't really know much at all.

# Late Round Positions Strategy

Don't overpay for defenses and kickers. Wait as long as possible to draft either a defense or a kicker. Most of the time, end up streaming both positions. Draft based on ADP and look at the best possible defense and kicker based on last year's statistics. Kickers are more consistent than defense.

Stray away from consensus rankings. If you follow the herd, you will not win. Every year mainstream analysts bring out lists that are literally last year's top finishers, copied and pasted to this year. The ADP is just a guideline - don't be afraid to stray away from the mainstream and think outside the box.

# Personal Enjoyment Factor

Draft players you like. It's important to remember fantasy football should be fun. When game days roll around, it's more enjoyable when the players you'd root for naturally are also putting up points for your fantasy squad. Fantasy football is supposed to be entertaining.
