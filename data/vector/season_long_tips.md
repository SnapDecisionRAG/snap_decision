# Waiver Wire System Fundamentals

The waiver wire is a ranking system that determines who gets priority at picking up free agent players each week. Once a manager claims a player via the waiver wire, they are sent down to the bottom of the priority list. While managers can claim multiple players in a week, each individual claim forces a manager back to the bottom of the priority order.

If you are unsuccessful in a claim attempt, it's either because someone with higher priority submitted a claim for the same player or because you don't have the roster space to make the claim. An unsuccessful claim will not alter your waiver priority.

# League-Mate Analysis Strategy

Nobody knows your friends better than you, and you can use this knowledge to your advantage when it comes to the waiver wire. Each week, take a look at teams ahead of you on the waiver wire to predict how they will use their priority. A quick glance at a league-mate's upcoming players on bye or injuries can show which position they will be attacking the most.

Know your league-mates' overall tendencies. You may have prior knowledge of whether your league-mates are very aggressive in claiming players or not. You may also know who is more likely to follow projected points by claiming players at the top of the list or who may be better at identifying sleepers.

# Waiver Wire Planning Strategy

One of the most important parts about the waiver wire is that an unsuccessful claim doesn't skip your turn and instead moves on to your next claim. Use this to maximize waiver wire efficiency by planning ahead for every scenario with multiple claims.

Set up claims to handle multiple outcomes: both players claimed, only one claimed, or neither claimed. By planning ahead for every scenario, you can guarantee maximum efficiency in your claims every week without making unnecessary drops.

# Streaming Strategy

A streamer is a player you only plan on using for the short term, generally one to three weeks. Quarterback, tight end, kicker, and defense are the main positions to stream because most leagues start only one of each position, making it more likely that managers don't carry backups.

Streaming can force managers into wasting high waiver priority on short-term players. Avoid this by adding players a week early after waivers clear. Research when your "onesie" position players are on bye and look at available players' schedules for the future to plan ahead and add your players the week before without wasting waiver priority.

# Long-Term Waiver Strategy

Playing the long game means realizing the fantasy football regular season is around 14 weeks in most leagues and conserving your resources for when you can best use them. Save your waiver priority for when you truly need to make a claim or when an incredible pickup opportunity presents itself.

Injuries happen very randomly, and waiting one more week to use your high priority could be a gamble worth considering. Always calculate your risk and consider adding your deeper targets after waivers clear because it could put you first in line for a splash player or injury opportunist in the future.

# FAAB Budget Management

In FAAB (Free Agent Acquisition Budget) leagues, every team gets a budget (usually $100) to spend on free agents throughout the season. Teams make blind bids, and the highest bidder wins the player. Once you spend your entire budget, your team can no longer add players during the blind bidding window for anything more than $0.

Many leagues allow teams to make $0 bids, which means you can add players for free, assuming no one else bids a dollar or more. Some leagues have a $1 minimum bid - those few extra dollars throughout the year could be the difference between landing a big target or missing out on a potential league-winner later.

# FAAB Spending Philosophy

Be aggressive with your budget early in the season and heading into the fantasy playoffs. The big-ticket waiver wire targets early in the year are typically the best options for the season. They can help your team for most of the year, while a player added in Week 13 only helps for a few weeks.

Don't be shy about spending 30-40% of your budget on a player you really like early in the year. Your FAAB is like a new car - it depreciates in value as the season progresses. The value of your money declines because there aren't as many appealing waiver wire targets once late in the season.

# FAAB Spending Guidelines

Don't spend FAAB budget on streaming positions. Don't use even $1 of your budget on a defense or kicker for the week. Add those players as free agents or for $0 bids only. The money you save is more valuable than the streaming player.

Similarly, don't spend FAAB on bye-week replacements, especially at tight end and quarterback positions. The player you'll use for one week isn't worth spending your budget on. However, quarterbacks and tight ends that you plan to hold onto for several weeks as high-upside additions are worth paying for like any other position.

Unless you plan on holding onto the player for multiple weeks as a rest-of-season addition, don't spend your FAAB on one-week replacement options.

# Post-Draft Roster Management

Your team isn't finished after the draft. Active managers win at a much higher rate than casual ones. Every single week, there is at least a player or two on waivers who is well worth adding to your roster, if not your starting lineup. One big injury can really shake things up in a hurry.

Team building is an ongoing process. Many fantasy managers don't maximize their chances at a league title because they are too content with their roster and afraid to shake things up. Focus on maximizing your starting lineup, especially as bye weeks begin and playoffs approach.

# Roster Flexibility Tactics

Drop your kicker and/or defense to hold a skill position player for a few extra days. As someone who often streams both positions, drop your kicker and defense when the week is complete so they are added to the available player pool, which slightly increases your chances of getting who you want off the wire.

Use roster spots on skill position players in uncertain situations heading into the week. If a star player is questionable for the upcoming week, grab their backup instead of a replaceable kicker or defense. If the starter plays, just cut the backup for a kicker/defense on Sunday. If the starter is out, you found yourself a potential starter.

# Trading Philosophy and Strategy

Trading is an ongoing process whether it's season-long, dynasty, or keeper leagues. The goal is simple: every trade should improve your team. The ideal trade has you sending away an overvalued player (selling high) and receiving an undervalued player (buying low).

Don't just consider what the other manager can do for you. Look closely at their roster and see if there is a void you can help them fill. Find a trading partner who is weak at a position where you are strong, and vice versa. This makes deals significantly more likely.

# Trade Evaluation Techniques

Put yourself in the other manager's shoes. Once you have a trade proposal in mind, flip the script and imagine you're on the other side. Think about whether you'd accept it, consider it, or laugh off the offer. If it's the latter, adjust the deal.

Cure trade fear with this simple trick: If someone offers you Player A for your Player B and you can't decide, imagine you already had Player A and were being offered Player B. Would you accept the deal now? This helps overcome the endowment effect, where we overvalue assets we possess.

# Trade Timing Strategy

Pick your spots when making offers. If there's positive hype about a player, wait a week or so until the industry is buzzing about someone else before making the move. On the other hand, if there's a negative report on a player and you're not buying it, that is the perfect opportunity to make an offer.

Don't get mad about trade offers, even if you don't like them. Just be happy you have someone in your league unafraid to keep the league active by making offers. The first offer is rarely the best offer - it's a starting point for negotiation.

# Value-Based Decision Making

During your draft, never allow biases, emotion, or need to stand in the way of maximizing value. Don't pass on obvious value, even if it's not a position of need. There are always players who consistently fall too far in drafts and often prove to be solid values.

Unless you're in a tournament, you don't need a home run with every single pick to win the title. You need to get to the playoffs first, and from there you need a competent lineup and some luck. Having a few reliable starters is key to achieving both goals.

# In-Season Lineup Management

Setting your lineup takes place weekly and involves deciding which players' scores will count for your matchup. You can only start as many players as your league settings allow and only in the positions in which each player is designated.

Always check your lineups for bye weeks and injuries before games start. Make changes early in the week rather than scrambling on Sunday morning. If you have multiple players on bye, you'll probably want to hit the waiver wire. Check injury designations: Out (O) means won't play, Doubtful (D) means unlikely to play, Questionable (Q) means uncertain and likely a game-time decision.

# Advanced Roster Construction

Focus on collecting the best talent possible rather than filling out a perfect lineup on draft day. Lineups look very different in the playoffs than they do entering Week 1. By drafting for value and staying active on waivers and trades, you can overcome early roster deficiencies.

Balance your need for reliable starters with high-upside bench players. You want players who can provide a weekly floor, but also stash players with breakout potential. The waiver wire often provides tremendous impacts, with players surging to the top of depth charts and fantasy rankings throughout the season.

# Bench Management Strategy

Your bench should be used as a feeder system for your starting lineup. Keep upside in focus when working the waiver wire - you can almost always find a player who projects for mediocre points, so don't prioritize those types when taking a shot on a free agent. Instead, think more long term.

That often means grabbing and stashing running backs, who can see huge spikes in fantasy value when injuries hit players in front of them on the depth chart. It can also mean young, talented players who haven't gotten the opportunity yet. However, there are cases where your waiver-wire focus should look short-term when you need immediate help.

# Trade Market Psychology

Trading in fantasy is one of the most polarizing aspects of the game, as most managers seem to either be extremely aggressive or extremely quiet. Understanding the philosophies, tendencies, and skill levels of your leaguemates is an edge you can use when evaluating the trade market.

Some managers overvalue their players so much that it's not worth your time to even negotiate. The fun of fantasy is that we don't all value players the same way. Unfortunately, it's very easy for managers to become offended if they believe you're trying to rip them off. Even if you get an offer you're not considering, be excited by the idea of someone being open for business.

# Injury and Opportunity Management

Every single week, there is at least a player or two on waivers who is well worth adding to your roster, if not your starting lineup. One big injury can really shake things up in a hurry. This applies to the weeks leading up to the season as well.

Use the roster spot on a skill position player in an uncertain situation heading into the week. If a star player is hurt and questionable for the upcoming week, grab their backup instead of a replaceable position player. If the starter plays, just cut the backup. If the starter is out, you found yourself a potential starter and can place them in an IR spot or cut someone else.

# Positional Streaming Advanced Tactics

Besides dropping kicker and defense to hold skill players, use this strategy during the period between your draft and the start of the season. Even if forced to draft a kicker and defense, drop them immediately afterward (assuming league rules allow it) and instead grab a high-upside insurance player to keep rostered until closer to Week 1.

There's little to lose and a lot to gain. There are players available late in drafts or on preseason and early-season waivers every single year. If you're active, you can recover from draft mistakes and still claw your way to a title.

# Value Recognition Principles

During the draft and throughout the season, focus on collecting the best talent possible rather than filling perceived needs. Don't pass on obvious value, even if it's not a position of need. There are always players who consistently fall too far and often prove to be solid values.

You don't need a home run with every single pick to win the title. You need to get to the playoffs first, and from there you need a competent lineup and some luck. Having reliable starters who consistently produce is key to achieving both goals, even if they're not the most exciting names.

# League Format Adaptations

Dynasty leagues require considering perceived value - managers love unproven youngsters and draft picks way more than established veterans, excluding obvious superstars. You can take advantage by going extremely young at the expense of winning short-term, then peaking when other managers' depreciating assets are fading.

In superflex leagues, quarterbacks are significantly more valuable. Move all quarterbacks up your draft board and plan to roster an extra player or two at the position. In FAAB leagues, be more aggressive early when the best players are available rather than saving money for later when options are limited.

# Season-Long Success Mindset

Active managers win at a much higher rate than casual ones. Some managers are rarely content and are constantly tinkering with trade offers and waivers to get one step closer to a title. Others attack waivers only when needed and are likely to ignore trade proposals.

It's possible to hit on nearly every draft pick and compete for a league title, but it's more likely that you'll need to make adjustments throughout the season. Fantasy points come in all shapes and sizes, and the teams that adapt and stay active throughout the season are the ones that consistently compete for championships.
